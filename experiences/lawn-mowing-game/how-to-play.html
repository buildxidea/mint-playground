Mowed How to play

Cut the perfect lawn

Five overgrown yards. One tank of fuel each. Mow every blade into clean parallel stripes — in the pattern the yard asks for — before you run dry.

The idea

  1. Drive over tall grass to cut it. Your mower cuts a lane exactly as wide as its deck. It turns on the spot rather than steering in an arc, so your lanes come out straight without any fighting.
  2. Cut grass lies down in the direction you drove. Grass laid toward the sun looks pale; grass laid away from it looks dark. That is the whole trick behind lawn stripes, and it is real — drive a lane, come back the other way, and the two lanes read light and dark side by side.
  3. Cover the yard in the pattern it asks for. Some yards want north–south lanes, some east–west, and the last one wants a crosshatch. Finish before the tank runs dry.

Sound

The engine tells you what the mower is doing. It idles when you stop, settles into a steady note while you cruise, revs noticeably higher when you hold Shift to boost, and bogs down when you are shoved against a tree. The hiss riding on top is the blades actually cutting — when it fades, you are driving over grass you have already mown and burning fuel for nothing.

Use the speaker button in the top right, or the sound row in settings, to mute. Your choice is remembered.

The rules

  • Coverage You need to cut almost all of the lawn — 95% in most yards. Patios, pools and flowerbeds are not lawn, so they never count against you. The yard ends automatically once there is nothing left worth chasing.
  • Pattern What counts is the direction each patch of grass was last cut in. Mow a patch north–south and later cross it east–west, and it now counts as east–west. On the crosshatch yard the rule inverts: a patch only counts once it has been cut in both directions.
  • Fuel One tank per yard, and no refills. Driving burns fuel, cutting tall grass burns more, boosting burns much more, and grinding against a tree burns most of all. Re-mowing grass you already cut costs fuel and earns you nothing, which is why tidy routing beats fast driving.
  • Running dry Not a game over. The yard simply ends where it is and you are graded on what you managed. Fall short of the coverage or pattern targets and you mow it again — nothing is lost but the time.
  • Obstacles Trees, gnomes, fences and flowerbeds stall the mower and waste fuel while you are shoved up against them. They never damage it, and you can never get permanently stuck.

Passing a yard needs both its coverage and its pattern target, not just a good overall score. Your best grade on each yard is remembered, and passing one unlocks the next.

Getting a better grade

  • Plan the turn, not the lane Lanes are easy. Fuel goes missing in the turns and in the awkward strips along fences and around trees. Decide where those go before you start rather than mopping them up at the end.
  • Alternate direction every lane It costs nothing, and it is what produces the light-and-dark banding that makes a lawn look properly mown.
  • Stop when you are done Once the lawn is cut, every extra second is fuel out of your score. Press F and bank it.
